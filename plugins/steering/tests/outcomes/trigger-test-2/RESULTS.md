# Trigger test 2: no arm separates, and the instrument is still the problem

Nine runs, three arms, twenty items. The pilot scored 19 of 20 and the gate opened. Scoring in
`./SCORING.md`, arms in `./ARMS.md`, runs in `./runs/`.

## The scores

| Arm | Capability | Third person | Total | Should-trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | no | no | 57/60 | 27/30 |
| B | yes | yes | 55/60 | 25/30 |
| C | no | yes | 52/60 | 22/30 |

The order is A, then B, then C. The pre-registration predicted B first.

No arm fired on a should-not item once, in any of the nine runs. Every error is a false negative.

## The verdict, and the discipline behind it

**No arm separates from another.** That is the pre-registration's third outcome.

The scorer refused the reading its own numbers invited. Arm A led arm B, and arm A breaks both rules
under test. That is the pre-registered "A beats B" outcome, which reads: our rules are worse than
the advice they replace.

It did not report that. The gap is 6.7 points on 30 should-trigger trials, and the design states
that a 5-point difference does not show at 30 trials. The gap is smaller than the difference the
design already disclaims.

It also corrected the pre-registration. The design claimed a 10-point difference shows reliably at
30 trials. On the counts observed, the exact test gives p = 0.53. The claim was too generous, and
the design was mine.

## What each comparison can carry

| Comparison | Isolates | Gap | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A against B | both rules together | 6.7 points | Below the floor. No difference. |
| B against C | the capability rule | 10.0 points | On the floor, p = 0.53. Not settled. |
| C against A | the third-person rule | 16.7 points | Above the floor, p = 0.18. A signal, and confounded. |

The one comparison that clears the floor runs against the rule. Arm C, written to our third-person
rule, scored lowest.

Two confounds sit on it, and both were named before the run. The imperative travels with the second
person: arm A says "Use this when" and arm C says "Applies when". And all six distractor
descriptions open "Use when", so arm A matches the collection's house form while arm C reads as
foreign to it.

So the result does not show that the pronoun causes the loss. It shows that the package we call the
third-person rule scored worst.

## Reliability, which is the sharper finding

| Arm | Runs | Spread | Answer vectors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 19, 19, 19 | 0 | all three identical, item for item |
| B | 18, 18, 19 | 1 | two identical |
| C | 19, 16, 17 | 3 | all three different |

Arm C's spread is larger than every between-arm difference except A over C.

A description returning 19, then 16, then 17 on identical input is not read the same way twice. Any
ranking resting on three runs of that arm is unsafe, and the B against C comparison rests on exactly
that.

Arm A returned the same twenty labels three times. Whatever else it does, the model reads it
consistently.

## The instrument, again

Ten boundary items scored 100 percent in all three arms. The four items built to carry the test
returned 35 of 36.

Test 1 hit a ceiling on the should-trigger half. Test 2 moved the ceiling to the should-not half and
to the discriminating items. The instrument still has no room to show a difference.

One item is dead. Item 7 was answered `none` by all nine runs and by the pilot. Arm B states that
it settles what to say back to the reviewer, and declined it four times. That is an item fault. It
caps every arm at 19 and separates nothing.

## What follows for the two rules

**The capability rule does not earn Blocking.** Two tests have now failed to find a consequence.
The four items built to test that rule returned 35 of 36 across every arm, including the arms that
break it. Our own rule says a Blocking severity needs a named consequence. This one has never named
one. Lowered to Important.

**The third-person rule is unresolved and confounded.** The only comparison above the floor runs
against it, and it cannot be separated from the imperative mood or from breaking form with the
surrounding collection. Both plausibly hurt. Settling it needs a fourth arm that holds mood constant.

## What a third test would need

More discriminating items, because 35 of 36 means these four discriminate nothing. A fourth arm
holding mood constant. Item 7 dropped or rewritten. Item 3 checked, at 5 of 9 with defensible
reasoning on both sides.

That is a larger test than this one, and this one cost nine runs.
