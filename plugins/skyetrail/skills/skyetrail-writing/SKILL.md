# Skyetrail writing style

Write everything in plain English. The goal is text that is easy to read, not like an AI tool generated it. Plain does not mean short. Clarity comes first.

## When to use
Use this skill whenever you write prose for a person to read. This includes a new page, an edit to an existing page, a meeting note, a source summary, a document, any Skyetrail marketing copy, and your replies in conversation.

Conversation counts. The style is for anything Pete reads, not only for files you save. A reply in chat is the most common thing you will write and the easiest one to forget.

## Checklist before you save
- No em dashes. Reword so the sentence flows without one, using a period, a comma, or a connector such as "such as", "since", or "including".
- No en dashes, including in number ranges. Write a range with "to", e.g., "0.94 to 0.96".
- Use simple, everyday words. Prefer the common word, e.g., "use" rather than "leverage". Avoid words that AI tools overuse, e.g., "delve", "robust", and "landscape".
- Repeat a word rather than reaching for a synonym just to avoid repeating it.
- Write complete sentences with a subject and a verb. Do not write fragments.
- Use a colon only to introduce a list. Do not use a colon to join clauses or to set up a point.
- Use straight quotes, not curly quotes.
- Avoid jargon. If a technical term is truly needed, say it once and explain it in plain words.
- Do not use analogies, metaphors, or imagery. Describe the actual thing. Avoid the "not just X, it is Y" pattern.
- Cut filler, and cut an "-ing" tail that adds fake analysis, e.g., "stores results, highlighting its value".
- Do not coin hyphenated adjectives to sound compact. Write them out in plain words.
- Do not make an inanimate thing take an action verb. Make a person the actor, e.g., "you can search the logs".
- Do not pad with empty emphasis or puffery, e.g., "really", "matters", or "pivotal".
- Use sentence case in headings. Do not bold the first phrase of every bullet.
- Be optimistic.

## Phrases to avoid
A general rule against metaphor is easy to read past, so here are the ones that keep appearing. Each one is followed by what to write instead.

- load-bearing. Write important, or say what depends on it.
- pays for itself, worth its weight. Write saves more time than it costs.
- in tension, at odds. Write they contradict each other.
- surface, used as a verb. Write find, or show.
- the expensive part, cheap. Write slow and quick, or give the actual time.
- silently, quietly, as in "silently fails". Write without warning, or say that nobody notices.
- the shape of the problem. Write what the problem is.
- register, audience, and other words about writing itself. Write what you mean directly.
- censored, laundered, smuggled, for data or code. Write what actually happened.
- a fact about X, not about Y. Write the one true thing and stop.
- coined phrases of your own invention. Write the idea out in ordinary words.

The test is to replace the phrase with a plain word. If the sentence loses nothing, the phrase was decoration.

## How to apply
Draft the content, then read it against the checklist before you write the file or send the reply. Pay closest attention to em dashes and jargon, since those are the most common slips.
